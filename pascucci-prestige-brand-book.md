# Pascucci Prestige Brand Book

**Version:** 1.1  
**Company:** Pascucci Prestige  
**Market:** San Antonio, Texas  
**Category:** Luxury and exotic vehicle rentals / private automotive concierge  
**Primary logo reference:** `src/assets/pp-logo-grad.png`

## 1. Brand Essence

Pascucci Prestige is a private automotive concierge for clients who want access to luxury and exotic vehicles without the friction of a traditional rental experience.

The brand should feel selective, controlled, discreet, and high-touch. It should never feel like a dealership, discount rental counter, loud exotic-car influencer page, or generic luxury lifestyle brand.

### Core Idea

> **Exceptional vehicles should come with an exceptional experience.**

### Positioning Statement

Pascucci Prestige gives San Antonio clients seamless access to a curated fleet of luxury and exotic vehicles, delivered with concierge-level service, clear expectations, and private-client polish.

### Brand Promise

> **Exotic without the friction.**

### Brand Line

> **Drive beyond ordinary.**

### Supporting Lines

- Luxury delivered.
- Your vehicle. Your schedule. Your arrival.
- Curated vehicles. Personal service.
- Reserved for your arrival.
- The drive begins before the engine starts.
- Performance without friction.
- Luxury without noise.

## 2. Brand Personality

Pascucci Prestige balances two energies:

### Performance

Motion, speed, machinery, precision, dramatic lighting, sculptural forms, and controlled tension.

### Prestige

Restraint, space, private service, editorial typography, quiet confidence, and concierge-level hospitality.

### Personality Words

- Bold
- Exotic
- Controlled
- Discreet
- Personal
- Refined
- Confident
- Modern

### What This Means Visually

Use cinematic vehicle imagery, spacious layouts, strong alignment, refined typography, thin metallic rules, and tightly controlled accents. Let the car and the service feel aspirational without turning the brand into spectacle.

## 3. Audience

### Primary Clients

- Executives and business travelers
- Hotel guests and destination visitors

- Luxury lifestyle renters
- Local clients planning memorable weekends or arrivals
- Corporate hospitality and client-entertainment buyers

## 4. Voice And Messaging

### Voice Attributes

**Bold:** Use decisive headlines.  
**Controlled:** Avoid hype. Let the vehicle and service carry the weight.  
**Discreet:** Sound private, polished, and professional.  
**Personal:** Write like a concierge, not a corporate system.  
**Precise:** Make rates, delivery details, policies, and timing clear.

### Writing Rules

Use short, active sentences. Prefer one strong idea per line or paragraph. Keep service details specific. Avoid excessive adjectives, slang, hyperbole, generic luxury cliches, and aggressive sales language.

### Preferred Copy

> Your Urus will be delivered to Hotel Emma at 4:00 PM, detailed and ready for your arrival.

### Avoid

> Get ready to experience the ultimate luxury ride of your dreams with unbeatable service!

### Messaging Priority

1. The vehicle
2. The experience
3. The convenience
4. The exclusivity
5. The personal service

### CTA System

Primary CTAs:

- View the Fleet
- Request a Reservation
- Reserve Your Vehicle
- Schedule Delivery
- Plan Your Arrival

Secondary CTAs:

- View Vehicle Details
- Ask About Availability
- Explore Delivery Options
- Contact Concierge

Avoid:

- Buy Now
- Hurry
- Act Fast
- Cheap
- Best Deal
- Lowest Price

## 5. Logo System

The Pascucci Prestige logo combines a gold speed-influenced **P** mark, a silver exotic-car silhouette, the gold **PASCUCCI** wordmark, and the **PRESTIGE** descriptor.

The current raster logo file is 1142 x 730 px. The primary mark should eventually be preserved as a vector master for signage, print, embroidery, foil, and large-format use.

### Primary Logo

Use the complete logo when brand recognition matters most:

- Website header or footer
- Proposal covers
- Rate cards
- Printed folders
- Partner one-sheets
- Paid social end cards
- Reservation confirmations

### Secondary Logo Uses

Create and maintain these approved variants:

- Full-color logo on black
- Full-color logo on warm ivory
- Gold-and-white logo on black
- Black-and-gold logo on warm ivory
- One-color white logo
- One-color black logo
- Icon-only gold speed mark
- Simplified horizontal wordmark

### Descriptor

Use the descriptor only when context is needed:

> **Luxury Auto Concierge**

The descriptor should be visually secondary. It should never compete with Pascucci Prestige.

### Clear Space

Maintain clear space equal to at least the height of the letters in **PRESTIGE** around the full logo. For the icon-only mark, maintain clear space equal to one third of the mark height.

### Minimum Size

Digital:

- Full logo: 180 px wide minimum
- Icon-only mark: 32 px wide minimum

Print:

- Full logo: 1.75 in wide minimum
- Icon-only mark: 0.4 in wide minimum

### Logo Rules

Do:

- Use the logo on black, charcoal, or warm ivory
- Preserve proportions
- Keep generous clear space
- Use high-resolution raster or vector files
- Keep the gold mark and wordmark visually premium

Do not:

- Stretch, compress, rotate, or rebuild the logo
- Add heavy glow, outline, bevel, or shadow effects
- Place it over busy vehicle details
- Change the metallic relationships casually
- Put gold text on bright white at small sizes
- Use the mark as a repeating pattern unless it is extremely subtle

## 6. Color System

The palette should feel automotive, editorial, and private. Black creates depth. Ivory gives warmth. Gold carries prestige. Red introduces controlled performance. Titanium keeps the system connected to the vehicle silhouette and metallic logo language.

### Core Palette

| Name | Hex | RGB | Primary Use |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Obsidian Black | `#090A0B` | 9, 10, 11 | Primary dark background |
| Carbon Charcoal | `#202225` | 32, 34, 37 | Panels, secondary dark surfaces |
| Warm Ivory | `#F1EEE7` | 241, 238, 231 | Light editorial background |
| Champagne Gold | `#B89A63` | 184, 154, 99 | Premium accent, rules, icons |
| Racing Red | `#A7191F` | 167, 25, 31 | CTA and performance accent |
| Titanium Gray | `#8C9094` | 140, 144, 148 | Secondary text and metallic balance |

### Logo-Informed Metallic Notes

The supplied logo contains warmer dark-gold tones around `#A08040`, brighter champagne highlights near `#D0A060`, graphite strokes near `#303030` to `#505050`, and silver tones near `#C0C0C0` to `#E0E0E0`.

Use this as guidance for gradients and finishes:

- Gold should lean champagne, bronze, and warm metallic.
- Silver should stay neutral and graphite, not blue.
- Avoid yellow gold, orange gold, and glitter effects.
- Use gradients only when they feel metallic and controlled.

### Usage Ratio

- Obsidian Black: 40%
- Warm Ivory: 30%
- Carbon Charcoal: 15%
- Champagne Gold: 8%
- Titanium Gray: 5%
- Racing Red: 2%

### Color Rules

- Gold is an accent, not a fill color for entire layouts.
- Red is a signal, not a brand wash.
- Do not use red and gold at equal strength.
- Keep light layouts warm rather than stark white.
- Use charcoal instead of flat mid-gray.
- Ensure body text contrast meets accessibility requirements.

## 7. Typography

Typography should combine editorial prestige with operational precision.

### Brand Serif

Use for hero headlines, major section titles, pull quotes, vehicle names, and campaign lines.

Preferred:

- Cormorant Garamond
- Bodoni Moda
- Canela
- Editorial New
- Libre Baskerville
- Georgia as a system fallback

Direction:

- High contrast
- Elegant proportions
- Large scale
- Generous line height
- No novelty automotive styling

### Brand Sans

Use for body copy, navigation, forms, service descriptions, website UI, emails, and client communication.

Preferred:

- Avenir Next
- Inter
- Helvetica Now
- Neue Haas Grotesk
- Montserrat as a fallback

### Utility Face

Use for pricing, specs, location labels, dates, category labels, and small uppercase UI.

Preferred:

- DIN Condensed
- Trade Gothic Condensed
- Roboto Condensed
- Bebas Neue for display-only use

### Hierarchy

Display:

```css
font-family: "Cormorant Garamond", "Bodoni Moda", Georgia, serif;
font-size: clamp(3rem, 8vw, 7rem);
font-weight: 500;
line-height: 0.96;
letter-spacing: 0;
```

H1:

```css
font-family: "Cormorant Garamond", Georgia, serif;
font-size: clamp(2.5rem, 5vw, 5rem);
font-weight: 500;
line-height: 1;
letter-spacing: 0;
```

Body:

```css
font-family: "Avenir Next", Inter, Arial, sans-serif;
font-size: 1rem;
font-weight: 400;
line-height: 1.6;
letter-spacing: 0;
```

Utility Label:

```css
font-family: "DIN Condensed", "Roboto Condensed", sans-serif;
font-size: 0.78rem;
font-weight: 700;
line-height: 1;
letter-spacing: 0.12em;
text-transform: uppercase;
```

### Typography Rules

- Use serif type for prestige and emotion.
- Use sans serif for clarity and service.
- Use condensed type sparingly for specs and labels.
- Do not use script fonts.
- Do not use decorative racing fonts.
- Avoid dense paragraphs and tiny legal-looking body copy.

## 8. Layout System

The Pascucci Prestige layout system is spacious, cinematic, and controlled.

### Principles

- Let vehicles occupy dominant visual space.
- Use strong vertical and horizontal alignment.
- Create quiet margins.
- Use thin gold rules as separators.
- Use red only as a small signal.
- Favor asymmetrical editorial balance.
- Keep text blocks concise.

### Grid

Digital:

- 12 columns
- 24 px desktop gutters
- 16 px tablet gutters
- 12 px mobile gutters

Print:

- 6 or 12 columns
- Wide external margins
- Clear content zones
- Thin internal rules

### Shape Language

Use:

- Long horizontal rules
- Thin vertical dividers
- Rectangular panels
- Wide cinematic crops
- Small status markers
- 4 to 8 px radius on digital cards

Avoid:

- Heavy rounded cards
- Bubble shapes
- Excessive diagonal cuts
- Carbon-fiber texture as decoration
- Loud racing stripes
- Overcrowded collage layouts

## 9. Photography And Art Direction

Photography should feel editorial, architectural, cinematic, and premium.

### Primary Subjects

- Low-angle vehicle photography
- Three-quarter front views
- Detail shots of wheels, stitching, keys, badges, consoles, and lighting
- Hotel, airport, residential, and event delivery moments
- Concierge handoff moments
- San Antonio settings used subtly

### Lighting

Use dramatic directional light, controlled highlights, wet pavement reflections, and deep contrast. Avoid extreme HDR, synthetic reflections, heavy lens flare, and oversaturated neon.

### People

People should feel well dressed, calm, confident, and secondary to the vehicle. Avoid exaggerated poses, celebration gestures, and obvious staged wealth cues.

### San Antonio References

Use Tower of the Americas, downtown skyline, River Walk architecture, historic luxury hotels, modern residential districts, private aviation, and Pearl District cues with restraint. The city should locate the brand, not turn the work into tourism advertising.

### Avoid

- Dealership lots
- White-background inventory photography
- Nightclub environments
- Watches, cigars, cash, and champagne cliches
- Unsafe driving scenes
- Overcrowded people shots
- Generic stock luxury imagery

## 11. Digital Product Direction

For internal tools, booking flows, dashboards, and customer-facing interfaces, Pascucci Prestige should feel premium but operationally clear.

### UI Principles

- Prioritize speed, clarity, and trust.
- Keep reservation paths simple.
- Avoid dense booking-engine hero forms.
- Use dark hero and ivory content sections.
- Use status colors with restraint.
- Make document, approval, payment, and delivery states unmistakable.
- Keep mobile workflows simple for inspections and handoffs.

### Component Direction

Buttons:

- Primary buttons use Racing Red with white text.
- Secondary buttons use transparent or ivory surfaces with dark text.
- Gold is best for labels, icons, dividers, and premium emphasis, not primary CTAs.

Cards:

- Use 4 to 8 px radius.
- Avoid nested cards.
- Keep shadows soft and functional.
- Use clear section titles and small utility labels.

Statuses:

- Red: blocked, urgent, or primary action
- Gold: warning, pending, premium label
- Titanium: neutral or secondary information
- Green/sage may be used only for operational completion, not brand expression

## 13. Print And Physical Brand

Pascucci Prestige should feel especially strong in print. The tactile system should communicate private-client service and controlled luxury.

### Core Materials

- Rate cards
- Rental folders
- Key envelopes
- Reservation cards
- Thank-you cards
- Hotel partner sheets
- Wedding partner sheets
- Corporate account sheets
- Vehicle placards

### Finish Recommendations

Use one or two premium finishes at a time:

- Soft-touch black stock
- Warm ivory uncoated stock
- Gold foil
- Blind emboss
- Spot UV on vehicle silhouette
- Silver foil as a secondary finish
- Small racing-red edge, rule, or seal
- Thick card stock

Avoid stacking every finish in one piece. Restraint is part of the brand.

### Print Production Notes

- Keep logos, body text, headings, page numbers, QR codes, and legal language editable/vector whenever possible.
- Use raster vehicle imagery at final physical size with print-resolution targets.
- Treat gold gradients in raster art as visual support, not a substitute for true foil or spot finish specs.
- Maintain strong contrast for body copy on dark stocks.

## 15. Do And Do Not

### Do

- Keep layouts spacious.
- Make the vehicle dominant.
- Use elegant serif headlines.
- Use gold sparingly.
- Use red as a controlled accent.
- Show real service moments.
- Emphasize delivery and convenience.
- Keep copy concise.
- Maintain a private, polished tone.
- Use San Antonio references subtly.

### Do Not

- Look like a dealership.
- Look like a discount rental company.
- Use excessive gold gradients.
- Use nightclub neon.
- Use loud racing graphics.
- Overcrowd layouts.
- Rely on wealth cliches.
- Use generic stock luxury scenes.
- Overuse vehicle specifications.
- Fill every surface with text.
- Use inconsistent icon styles.
- Distort the logo.

---

## 16. Brand Quality Checklist

Before publishing any Pascucci Prestige material, check:

- Does the vehicle or concierge service feel like the hero?
- Is the tone confident without being loud?
- Is gold used with restraint?
- Is red used only as a signal or CTA?
- Is the logo legible with enough clear space?
- Does the layout feel spacious and controlled?
- Is the copy specific, short, and service-oriented?
- Does the work avoid dealership, discount, and influencer luxury cues?
- Are all important details readable on mobile and in print?
- Does the piece reinforce exotic access without friction?

## 17. Final Standard

Pascucci Prestige should feel like a private automotive concierge: curated, fast, polished, discreet, personal, and memorable.

The brand is not about showing off. It is about arrival, access, and trust.

> **Drive beyond ordinary. Exotic without the friction.**
