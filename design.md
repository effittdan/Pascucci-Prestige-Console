# Pascucci Prestige Design System

**Version:** 1.0  
**Brand:** Pascucci Prestige  
**Primary Market:** San Antonio, Texas  
**Category:** Luxury and exotic vehicle rentals / private automotive concierge

---

## 1. Brand Overview

Pascucci Prestige is a luxury and exotic vehicle rental company built around private access, premium service, and a highly curated client experience.

The brand should feel less like a traditional rental counter and more like a private automotive concierge.

### Brand Position

Pascucci Prestige delivers luxury and exotic vehicle access in San Antonio with concierge-level service. The experience is designed to feel elevated from the first inquiry through vehicle delivery, use, and return.

### Core Promise

> **Exotic without the friction.**

### Brand Mission

To give clients seamless access to extraordinary vehicles through a rental experience that is fast, polished, discreet, and personal.

### Brand Experience

Every interaction should communicate:

- Access
- Ease
- Confidence
- Exclusivity
- Precision
- Hospitality

Airport, hotel, residential, and special-event delivery should feel seamless and personalized.

The brand should offer access to vehicles and experiences that feel uncommon, aspirational, and memorable.

### Discretion

Client privacy, trust, and professionalism are central to the experience.

## 5. Brand Voice

### Voice Attributes

#### Bold

Use decisive language. Headlines should feel confident and direct.

#### Controlled

Avoid overstatement. Let the vehicle, photography, and service speak with restraint.

#### Discreet

The tone should feel private and polished, never showy or boastful.

#### Personal

Communication should feel attentive, human, and concierge-led.

#### Precise

Rates, specifications, delivery details, and policies should be presented clearly.

### Writing Style

Use:

- Short, active sentences
- Clear service language
- Minimal punctuation
- Strong headlines
- Refined descriptions
- Specific vehicle and experience details
- Confident calls to action

Avoid:

- Excessive adjectives
- Slang
- Hyperbole
- Generic luxury clichés
- Aggressive sales language
- Overly casual phrasing
- Corporate jargon
- Long, dense paragraphs

### Tone Examples

#### Preferred

> Your Urus will be delivered to your hotel at 4:00 PM, detailed and ready for your arrival.

#### Avoid

> Get ready to experience the ultimate luxury ride of your dreams with unbeatable service!

## 6. Logo System

### Primary Logo

The primary Pascucci Prestige logo includes:

- The stylized gold “P” speed mark
- The silver exotic-car silhouette
- The gold **PASCUCCI** wordmark
- The white or silver **PRESTIGE** descriptor

The complete logo should remain the primary brand identifier.

### Recommended Logo Variations

- Full-color logo on black
- Full-color logo on warm ivory
- Gold-and-white logo on black
- Black-and-gold logo on warm ivory
- Single-color white logo
- Single-color black logo
- Icon-only “P” speed mark
- Simplified horizontal wordmark

### Secondary Descriptor

When needed, use:

> **Luxury Auto Concierge**

This descriptor should remain visually secondary to the Pascucci Prestige name.

### Logo Clear Space

Maintain clear space around the logo equal to at least the height of the letter **P** in **PRESTIGE**.

No text, imagery, trim lines, or page edges should enter this area.

### Minimum Size

For digital use:

- Full logo: minimum width of 180 px
- Icon-only mark: minimum width of 32 px

For print:

- Full logo: minimum width of 1.75 in
- Icon-only mark: minimum width of 0.4 in

### Logo Usage Rules

Do:

- Use the logo on black, charcoal, or warm ivory backgrounds
- Maintain generous clear space
- Preserve the original proportions
- Use high-resolution or vector files
- Keep the mark visually dominant

Do not:

- Stretch or compress the logo
- Rebuild the logo in unrelated fonts
- Add heavy glow effects
- Apply excessive drop shadows
- Place the logo over visually busy areas
- Change the gold, silver, and white relationships without approval
- Add gradients that reduce legibility
- Outline the logo
- Rotate the mark

## 7. Color Palette

### Primary Colors

| Color | Hex | RGB | Usage |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Obsidian Black | `#090A0B` | 9, 10, 11 | Primary background, premium applications |
| Carbon Charcoal | `#202225` | 32, 34, 37 | Secondary background, panels, cards |
| Warm Ivory | `#F1EEE7` | 241, 238, 231 | Light background, editorial layouts |
| Champagne Gold | `#B89A63` | 184, 154, 99 | Premium emphasis, rules, icons, typography |
| Racing Red | `#A7191F` | 167, 25, 31 | Performance accent, calls to action |
| Titanium Gray | `#8C9094` | 140, 144, 148 | Supporting text, metallic balance |

### Color Philosophy

The palette should feel automotive, editorial, and premium.

- Black provides confidence and depth.
- Charcoal softens large dark surfaces.
- Ivory adds warmth and editorial sophistication.
- Gold signals exclusivity.
- Red introduces controlled performance energy.
- Titanium reflects the metallic language of the logo and vehicles.

### Recommended Usage Ratio

- Obsidian Black: 40%
- Warm Ivory: 30%
- Carbon Charcoal: 15%
- Champagne Gold: 8%
- Titanium Gray: 5%
- Racing Red: 2%

### Color Rules

- Champagne gold should be used sparingly.
- Racing red should never become the dominant brand color.
- Avoid bright yellow gold.
- Avoid large red background fields.
- Avoid combining gold and red at equal visual weight.
- Keep light layouts warm, not stark white.
- Use charcoal rather than pure medium gray.

## 8. Typography

Typography should balance editorial luxury with automotive precision.

Preferred:

- Cormorant Garamond

- Canela
- Editorial New

Use for:

- Hero headlines
- Campaign lines
- Pull quotes
- Vehicle names
- Major section titles

Recommended characteristics:

- High contrast
- Elegant proportions
- Moderate letter spacing
- Large scale
- Generous line height

### Body Sans Serif

Preferred:

- Avenir Next
- Inter
- Helvetica Now
- Neue Haas Grotesk
- Montserrat as a fallback

Use for:

- Body copy
- Navigation
- Service descriptions
- Forms
- Digital interfaces
- Client communications

### Utility / Specification Typeface

Preferred:

- DIN Condensed
- Trade Gothic Condensed
- Roboto Condensed
- Bebas Neue for display-only applications

Use for:

- Rates
- Vehicle specifications
- Location labels
- Dates
- Delivery details
- Category labels
- Small uppercase navigation

### Typography Hierarchy

#### Display

```css
font-family: "Cormorant Garamond", "Bodoni Moda", Georgia, serif;
font-size: clamp(3rem, 8vw, 7rem);
font-weight: 500;
line-height: 0.95;
letter-spacing: -0.02em;
```

#### H1

```css
font-family: "Cormorant Garamond", Georgia, serif;
font-size: clamp(2.5rem, 5vw, 5rem);
font-weight: 500;
line-height: 1;
```

#### H2

```css
font-family: "Cormorant Garamond", Georgia, serif;
font-size: clamp(2rem, 3vw, 3.5rem);
font-weight: 500;
line-height: 1.08;
```

#### Body

```css
font-family: "Avenir Next", Inter, Arial, sans-serif;
font-size: 1rem;
font-weight: 400;
line-height: 1.6;
```

#### Utility Label

```css
font-family: "DIN Condensed", "Roboto Condensed", sans-serif;
font-size: 0.8rem;
font-weight: 600;
line-height: 1;
letter-spacing: 0.14em;
text-transform: uppercase;
```

### Typography Rules

- Use serif type for emotion and prestige.
- Use sans serif for clarity and function.
- Use condensed type for specifications and utility.
- Avoid using more than three type families in one composition.
- Avoid script fonts.
- Avoid decorative automotive fonts.
- Avoid overly tight body copy.
- Maintain generous white space around large headlines.

## 9. Layout System

### Overall Direction

Layouts should feel spacious, cinematic, and controlled.

### Core Layout Principles

- Use strong vertical and horizontal alignment.
- Create generous margins.
- Let vehicles occupy significant visual space.
- Avoid crowded text blocks.
- Use thin gold rules as separators.
- Use red only as a small signal or marker.
- Favor asymmetrical editorial balance over rigid symmetry.
- Allow negative space to create prestige.

### Grid

Recommended digital grid:

- 12 columns
- 24 px gutters on desktop
- 16 px gutters on tablet
- 12 px gutters on mobile

Recommended print grid:

- 6 or 12 columns
- Wide external margins
- Thin internal rules
- Clear content zones

### Shape Language

Use:

- Long horizontal rules
- Thin vertical dividers
- Rectangular panels
- Wide cinematic crops
- Minimal icon circles
- Soft corner radii on digital cards
- Shield and keyhole motifs only when subtle

Avoid:

- Heavy rounded cards
- Bubble shapes
- Excessive diagonal cuts
- Carbon-fiber textures used as decoration
- Busy geometric overlays
- Racing-stripe clichés

## 18. Brand Do and Don’t

### Do

- Keep layouts spacious
- Make the vehicle dominant
- Use elegant serif headlines
- Use gold sparingly
- Use red as a controlled accent
- Show real service moments
- Emphasize delivery and convenience
- Keep copy concise
- Maintain a private, polished tone
- Use San Antonio references subtly

### Don’t

- Look like a dealership
- Look like a discount rental company
- Use excessive gold gradients
- Use nightclub neon
- Use loud racing graphics
- Overcrowd layouts
- Rely on wealth clichés
- Use generic stock luxury scenes
- Overuse vehicle specifications
- Fill every area with text
- Use inconsistent icon styles
- Distort the logo

---

## 19. Accessibility

Premium design should remain readable and functional.

### Digital Requirements

- Maintain a minimum contrast ratio of 4.5:1 for body text.
- Do not use gold for small text on ivory.
- Do not use red as the only indicator of status.
- Use descriptive alt text for vehicle photography.
- Ensure buttons remain clear at mobile sizes.
- Keep body text at 16 px or larger.
- Provide visible focus states.
- Avoid placing text over busy vehicle details.

### Accessible Color Pairings

Preferred:

- Ivory on Obsidian
- Obsidian on Ivory
- White on Charcoal
- Obsidian on Champagne Gold for large labels
- White on Racing Red for buttons when contrast passes
